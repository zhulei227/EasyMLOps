4th Place Solution: Why "Less is More" (Stumps & Rank Ensembling)

A strict "Raw is Law" pipeline utilizing max_depth=2 Trees, RealMLP Student Distillation, and Rank Averaging to hit 0.95534 Private LB.

Predicting Heart Disease

Solution Writeup · 4th place · Mar 1, 2026

fullscreen

This dataset presented a unique challenge: the signal was nearly linear, and the associated synthetic data generation mechanisms heavily penalized over-engineering. My journey to the 4th Place Private LB (0.95534) was defined by stripping away complexity, strictly monitoring cross-validation gaps, and utilizing rank ensembling to secure a stable final prediction.

Here is a detailed, technical breakdown of my solution, structured through the lifecycle of the actual competition journey.

1. Defining the Core Challenge: A Linear Signal in Synthetic Noise

The objective was to predict the presence of Heart Disease based on 13 clinical features. However, the data provided was synthetic—generated by a deep learning model trained on a foundational, real-world heart disease dataset.

Early probing revealed that a basic Logistic Regression model combined with One Hot Encoding (expanding 13 categorical features into 449 dimensions) performed incredibly well (CV 0.95550, LB 0.95371). This confirmed that the primary predictive boundaries were near-linear and relatively clean. This insight fundamentally shaped my subsequent approach: avoid aggressive non-linear transformations and trust the native features.

2. Strategic Data Acquisition: The Role of the Original Dataset

Because the competition data was synthesized, utilizing the original real-world dataset (which the generative model was trained on) was a critical strategic lever.

Adversarial Validation: Before incorporating external data, I verified the integrity of the provided synthetic datasets. An adversarial validation between the train and test sets yielded an AUC of 0.501, confirming a flawless distribution match with zero drift.
Split Data Strategy: Because Neural Networks and Gradient Boosted Trees process synthetic noise differently, I implemented a bifurcated approach for integrating the Original Dataset, ensuring optimal signal extraction for each specific architecture.
3. Data Preparation: The "Raw is Law" Philosophy

Rather than generating exhaustive polynomial interactions, I adopted a strict "Raw is Law" approach. My data preparation pipeline was intentionally divergent, tailored specifically to the individual learning dynamics of Trees versus Neural Networks.

Architecture	Core Strategy	What Worked	What Failed
Tree-Based (GBDT)	Target Encoding & Augmentation	Out-of-fold target probability + frequency encoding (+0.00028 LB). Appending original dataset as training rows.	Generating 800+ explicit interaction polynomials (crashed local CV due to collinearity noise).
Neural Networks	Group Statistic Injection	Utilizing original dataset purely as an anchor to inject aggregated statistics (mean, std, skew) for context.	Appending original data as actual training rows (completely confused the Deep Network).
4. Model Architecture: Pitting Stumps Against Neural Networks

To construct an elite final ensemble, it was imperative to build an uncorrelated base layer consisting of models that leverage fundamentally different methods of learning.

Model Framework	Key Concept	Impact & Rationale
Gradient Boosted Stumps	Trees restricted to max_depth=2 + One Hot Encoding.	Cleanly isolated category levels without overfitting the data's synthetic noise. Consistent local CV of ~0.95574.
Periodic Embedding MLPs	Sine/cosine transformations (dim=8) for numerical features.	Bypassed the "spectral bias" that typically causes standard deep neural networks to struggle on tabular data.
RealMLP Averaging	Internal 8-model simultaneous training.	Greatly stabilized the network's predictions by averaging discrete sub-models (256 batch size, 100 epochs) before output.
RealMLP Architecture Flow

The model's pipeline was distinctly separated to handle the dual nature of tabular inputs before converging into the deep network:

Input Stage	Processing Step	Output Shape
Numerical Features	Mapped via Periodic Embeddings (sines & cosines) to bypass spectral bias.	dim = 8 per feature
Categorical Features	Passed directly into One Hot Encoding.	Sparse Vectors
Concatenation	Both streams merged into the primary deep network block.	Dense Tensor
Deep Network	4-layer MLP · 384 units per layer · Mish activation.	Raw Probability
Output Averaging	The architecture internally trains 8 discrete models simultaneously and averages them.	Final Stabilized Prediction
5. Evaluation Strategy: Gap Tracking to Prevent Overfitting

While trusting local Cross-Validation (CV) is standard practice, addressing the synthetic generative noise in this dataset required an additional layer of evaluation strictness.

Method: I utilized a standard 5-Fold Stratified K-Fold cross-validation strategy (seed 42) to maintain consistency.
Gap Tracking: I discovered that high-CV models (like CatBoost) could aggressively overfit the generative noise. Consequently, I religiously tracked the OOF-to-Public LB Gap. A healthy gap was empirically determined to be ~0.00185. If a local CV improvement caused this marginal gap to widen beyond -0.00190, the experiment was instantly discarded to prevent Private LB shake-up.
6. The Optimization Journey: From Champion Models to Rank Ensembling

This crucial phase details the precise trial-and-error progression mapping the transition from strong standalone models to the final 4th place submission composite.

Optimization Phase	The Challenge / Concept	The Execution / Adjustment	The Result
Phase 1: The OOF Trap	Unconstrained optimizer assigned ~65% weight to CatBoost due to high OOF AUC, overfitting and tanking Public LB.	Manually capped tree models at 35% weight. Strictly blended only elite champion models (>0.9538 solo).	Proved that in this competition, Purity > Diversity.
Phase 2: Distillation	Wanted to extract the signal of a complex blend (Teacher Ensemble) into a stable standalone model.	Extracted Test Set predictions from the Teacher >99% or <1% confidence (~48,000 Hard Labels). Trained a new Student NN on Train + Hard Test rows.	The Student NN smoothed jagged decision boundaries, becoming my strongest standalone CV model.
Phase 3: The Final Masterstroke (Rank Ensembling)

For my final selection, I completely abandoned raw probability blending.

Through various techniques, I generated four top-tier, highly optimized probability blends. However, averaging their raw probabilities is risky because marginal calibration differences can aggressively skew the mean prediction.

The Fix: I converted the raw probability predictions of all four blends into relative rankings, averaged those ranks, and normalized them back to [0,1].

The table below tracks the journey of my top component blends and how Rank Averaging created the ultimate shield against the Private LB shake-up.

Stage / Component	CV (OOF)	Public LB	Private LB
Teacher Ensemble (RealMLP 60% + CatBoost 40%)	0.95580	0.95396	0.95535
Distillation Student (Pseudo-labeled NN)	0.95567	0.95397	0.95531
Power-Averaged Blend (Sharpened)	0.95579	0.95397	0.95535
Apex Blend	0.95572	0.95397	0.95535
High-Purity Blend (CatBoost capped 35%)	0.95578	0.95398	0.95535
Selected Rank Blend (Average of Ranks)	0.9557X	0.95398	0.95534
Rank Ensembling Flow

To bypass absolute probability calibration issues entirely, I transitioned my final step from a mathematical mean to an ordered ranking aggregation:

Blend Protocol	Methodology & Action	Result
Step 1: Acquire Champion Blends	Extracted my Top 4 highly-optimized, independent probability blends (High-Purity, Apex, Distilled, Power-Averaged).	4 Raw Probability Vectors
Step 2: Rank Conversion	Converted every prediction vector into a relative ranking (1 to $N$), ignoring outlier confidence scores.	4 Rank Vectors
Step 3: Rank Aggregation	Averaged the 4 corresponding rank values for every row in the test set.	1 Consensus Rank Vector
Step 4: Probability Normalization	Scaled the final consensus ranks linearly back into the [0, 1] probability space required for AUC.	0.95534 Private LB

Rank averaging completely targets relative order, ignoring absolute probability calibration and outlier confidence. This mathematical stabilization created an incredibly robust submission that successfully insulated my models from the Public-to-Private distribution variance.
