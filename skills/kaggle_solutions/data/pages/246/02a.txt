[ my ensemble solution ]

Updated! please refer to the ppt and xls in the attachment for full details.

I present my solution , which is part of team "Convoluted predictions". I submitted an ensemble of 6 models (combine.0, combine.1, combine.2 combine.5, combine.5-max, combine.5-mix). This ensemble has LB=0.787.

There is one model that is discarded and not used (combine.4, single base network), but nevertheless gives good results.

Credits go to my teammate @Miha Skalic who discover the fast way to train the model in 2 stages. By training on the extracted features, it is much more faster and accurate!

.

First, I introduce combine.2. It uses gated scaling in the combination network fcnet3.

https://kaggle2.blob.core.windows.net/forum-message-attachments/257777/8063/c2.png

https://kaggle2.blob.core.windows.net/forum-message-attachments/257777/8064/fcnet3.png

How to train fcnet3:

Train the base networks (se-resent101 and xception) image wise.
Extract the features for each train image and store in disk (quantised to np.uint8 and store as memmap)
train fcnet3 product wise.

Tricks in training:

for base networks , use dropout and use augmentation=small shift, scale, rotation + flip + rotate90,180,270. Batch_size = 512 to 768, num_epoches = about 20. SDG/learning rate=0.01,0.001,0.0001 (manually tuned)

time to train (4x1080Ti) = 5 days per base networks

for fcnet3, use dropout and i only use augmentation=flip (due to lack of space in my SSD drive). Batch_size = 4096, num_epoches = about 100.SDG/learning rate=0.01,0.001,0.0001 (manually tuned)

time to train (4x1080Ti) = 1.5 days

With my gated Fcnet3, you can use low performance base network in the range of LB=0.69 to 0.71.

No. The adding is done for 4 images per product. After adding, one product has 2x2048 (2 base network) dim. This will make results independent of image order, see this paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00593.
The scaling is a gate to select how much to add from each of the 4 image.

"PointNet: Deep Learning on Point Sets for 3D Classification and Segmentation"
